The basics

Four things to get right.

Best formats

Print-ready PDF with fonts outlined is best. AI, EPS and SVG work too. For photos, use TIFF or a high-quality JPG.

Resolution

Banners are seen from a distance, so they need fewer pixels than you'd think. See the table below for your banner.

Safe area

Keep text and logos 5cm from every edge. Eyelets, hems and scaffold pockets sit in that border.

Colour

Supply CMYK if you can. RGB is fine, but very bright on-screen colours may print slightly softer.

Resolution

How sharp is sharp enough?

Measured at full printed size. The further away people stand, the lower the resolution you need.

Viewed fromTypical useAim for
Under 1mRoller banners, receptions150 DPI
1 to 3mShopfronts, events, fences100 DPI
Over 3mScaffolding, roadside, buildings50 to 72 DPI

Example: a 3 × 1m banner at 100 DPI needs an image about 11,800 × 3,900 pixels. Vector files (PDF, AI, EPS, SVG) stay sharp at any size.

Safe area

Leave room at the edges.

Keep anything important inside the dashed line. The outer 5cm is where hems are folded, eyelets are punched and scaffold pockets are sewn.

Before you upload

Quick checklist.

  • Artwork is the same size as your banner, or the same shape so it scales up
  • Fonts are outlined or embedded
  • Text and logos are at least 5cm from every edge
  • Photos are high resolution for how far away people will stand
  • Colours are CMYK if possible
  • File is PDF, AI, EPS, SVG, TIFF, PNG or JPG, under 250 MB
